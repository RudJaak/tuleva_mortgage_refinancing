# Research: Estonian mortgage market

_Researched 2026-10-09. Sources are primary where possible: Eesti Pank, FI and the ECB. Figures labelled "calc." are our own, derived from ECB MIR minus 6M Euribor._

## Headline for the pitch

**Estonian borrowers pay about 1 pp more over Euribor than Finnish borrowers, and the gap is persistent.**

ECB MIR rates on total new business in August 2026, minus 6M Euribor (calc.):

| Country | All-in rate | Implied spread |
|---|---|---|
| Finland | 3.24% | **≈ 0.5 pp** |
| Estonia | 4.26% | **≈ 1.55 pp** |
| Lithuania | 4.16% | ≈ 1.45 pp |
| Latvia | 4.39% | ≈ 1.7 pp |

The December 2025 picture was the same: Finland ≈ 0.7 pp, Estonia ≈ 1.6 pp. The euro-area average isn't comparable because most euro-area loans are fixed-rate.

On a €150k loan, 1 pp is about **€1,500 a year** in extra interest, before amortisation (calc.).

## Rates and margins

- **More than 93% of new loans are variable-rate**, almost all on **6M Euribor + a margin fixed for the life of the loan**. The same holds in Latvia and Finland; the euro-area share is about 15%.
- **New-loan margin history (Eesti Pank):**

  | Period | Margin |
  |---|---|
  | Before 2014 | ~2.2% |
  | ~2019–2020 | ~2.5% |
  | End-2022 | > 1.8% |
  | April 2024 | just under 1.5% (~1.6% excluding 0%-margin green loans) |
  | June 2025 | "lowest in ten years" |

- Eesti Pank's Financial Stability Review 1/2026 says competition "has no longer significantly affected the margin over the past year". The big three banks are defending market share.
- **Implied new-loan margin (calc.):** Jan 2024 ≈ 2.0 → Dec 2024 ≈ 1.6 → Jun 2025 ≈ 1.7 → Dec 2025 ≈ 1.6 → Aug 2026 ≈ 1.7 pp.
- **Margin on the outstanding stock: there is no published official figure.** Legacy loans at 1.85–2.2% is plausible, given past new-loan margins of 1.8–2.5%, but it is an **estimate**. The Eesti Pank statistics database is the place to verify it.
- Current offers from Estonian banks: 1.35–1.6% (green loans and campaigns); see `03-data-and-risk-classes.md`.

## Structure and size

**Mortgage market shares (FI, end-2025):**

| Bank | Share |
|---|---|
| **Swedbank** | **40%** |
| **SEB** | **27%** |
| LHV | 14% |
| Luminor | 8% |
| Coop | 6% |
| Bigbank | 3% |
| Others | 2% |

The top two hold 67% and the top three 81%. Estonia's banking sector is among the three most concentrated in the EU (HHI > 2000; Eesti Pank working paper TP 1/2024).

**Size:**

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Housing-loan stock | **€13.1bn** at end-2025 (€11.9bn end-2024); growing > 10% a year, over 3× the euro area |
| Households with a housing loan | **~192,000** at end-2024 (Eesti Pank). €13.1bn ÷ €67.8k average balance gives ≈ 193k loans |
| Average outstanding balance | **€67,815** (end-2025) |
| New lending in 2025 | €2.4bn across 17,200 contracts |
| Average new loan | €147.4k, 25.1-year term (Q4 2025) |

## Switching behaviour

- **Few borrowers move to another bank.**
  - In 2022, 361 loans moved bank, equal to 1.4% of that year's new-loan volume (Eesti Pank TP 1/2024).
  - The bill's explanatory memo says ~2% (2023) and "just under 10%" (2024), but it doesn't define the base.
  - Refinancing drove the record new lending in Q2 2025 (Postimees).
- **Many borrowers renegotiate with their own bank instead (calc.).** In 2025, renegotiations were ≈ **€1.9bn, about 40% of total new business**.
  - **Implication for L6:** a credible collective outside bid can trigger repricing at the incumbent bank. That is still a win for the member, but the pool's bargaining power drops unless members commit.
- **Cost of switching.** Official example from the bill's memo: €150k loan, 20 years, 4.5% rate.

  | Item | Cost |
  |---|---|
  | Early-repayment fee (3 months' interest) | €1,683 |
  | Valuation | €280 |
  | New contract fee (~1%, some banks 0.4%) | €1,500 |
  | Notary | €275 |
  | State fee | €75 |
  | **Total** | **€3,813 (2.5% of the loan)** |

  - The early-repayment fee is avoidable with ~3 months' notice.
  - A pooled tender could negotiate the **contract fee and the valuation away**, because banks run campaigns that waive them.
- **Bill 864 SE** (no notary needed when only the lender changes):
  - Eesti Pank and FI proposed it in February 2024.
  - The government approved it on 2 April 2026, and it was submitted on 6 April 2026.
  - The first reading is done.
  - **There is no record of adoption as of 2026-10-09.** The planned 1 July 2026 start date has passed.
  - The Chamber of Notaries opposes it.
  - Other Eesti Pank and FI proposals: banks doing their own valuations, a state-fee exemption, and 3–5-year fixed periods.

## Profitability and politics

- Estonian banks' return on equity is ~12.5%, against an OECD average of ~8%.
  - TP 1/2024 concludes that competition is weaker than in many developed countries, which shows up as higher loan prices and persistent profits.
  - Bank ROEs: LHV 18.2% (Q4 2025), Coop 12.9% (2025).
- Bank taxes:
  - The bank advance income tax rose from 14% to 18% from 2025.
  - A proposed solidarity tax was not adopted.
- The 2025–2027 coalition agreement, point 311, commits to "increase competition in the housing loan market and reduce loan costs". **This makes the political wind favourable.**
- Konkurentsiamet has published no mortgage market study. The de facto study is Eesti Pank TP 1/2024.

## Funding and precedents

- **Covered bonds** are among banks' cheapest funding after demand deposits. Three Estonian banks issue them:
  - LHV: €300m, 4 years, 2.68% coupon (June 2025), 2.5× oversubscribed.
  - Coop: debut issue in 2025, with EBRD taking €20m.
  - Luminor.
  - About €4–5bn is outstanding (approximate). **There is no Estonian RMBS.**
- **A mortgage at Euribor + ~1.6 pp is an attractive yield next to covered bonds.** That supports a later pension-money-backed vehicle.
- **Sweden, pension money to mortgage funds** (the closest precedent for L6's fund stage):
  - In 2022 the Folksam group put SEK 10bn into the Stabelo and Hypoteket mortgage funds; KPA Pension contributed SEK 5bn.
  - **Caution:** Swedbank bought Stabelo in 2025 for ~SEK 350m. Commentators called it a failure of the deposit-free fund model.
- **Swedish unions** have union-member discounts of about −0.10 pp (e.g. Swedbank for LO, SEB for SACO/TCO). These are list-price discounts, not portfolio tenders.
- **Tuleva** has no published mortgage or insurance joint-procurement track record.
  - Its precedent is policy advocacy, e.g. a ban on pension-switching fees worth ~€1.5m a year.
  - The hackathon themes ("sensible borrowing", "collective purchasing power") come from a member survey (Tuleva podcast #14, 15 Sep 2026).
- Finland's Hypo (member-owned) and the Danish balance principle are relevant models, but **we have not verified them** yet.
